ECHO Listener Instructions

A guide to the three research activities

A Conversational System for Generating Emotionally Coherent Responses in Text and Speech Modalities

Version 2 10 September 2026

What your participation helps us learn

We are studying whether computer-generated speech expresses an intended emotion while sounding natural. ECHO brings together a language model, which writes a reply, and a text-to-speech system, which voices it. In a conversation, your selected emotion tells the system what tone to aim for. We evaluate how this integration works; we are not asking you to assess a person's mental state.

Your task is to report your own impressions. You may hear a clear emotion, mixed expression or little emotion. All of these are useful observations. There are no preferred answers, and a high score does not make you a better participant.

Follow the activities in this order

  1. Listening test: hear 45 recorded clips with system names hidden and rate each one. This is the main controlled listening activity.
  2. Test mode: after Listening, check Happy, Angry, Sad and Relaxed in all five available engines using the same phrase, and rate every message.
  3. Explore Mode: check all four emotions in all three conversation engines; rate each voice before assessing its transcript.

Complete only the stages the researcher assigns. You may decline the later activities. Read the sections through Listening questions before Listening. Open the Test and Explore sections after Listening, or when the researcher confirms that you are participating only in the application activities. Do not preview named systems before the blind test.

Before opening your study link

Read the participant information sheet, including the researcher contact, data arrangements and withdrawal procedure. Ask the researcher if any of this is unclear before you consent. This instruction guide accompanies that sheet.

Use headphones in a quiet place. Set a comfortable volume and keep it unchanged while comparing clips. Listen at normal speed. You must be 18 or older and comfortably understand spoken English for this study. You may pause or stop without giving a reason.

Keep the same device and browser during a session. For the complete set of activities, use a desktop computer with a current browser, such as Chrome or Edge. If your device is unsuitable, tell the researcher rather than changing the assigned task.

Enter ECHO

Open the link supplied by the researcher. It may take you directly to Listening; keep any group assignment in that link. If you see the homepage, enter your first name and a nickname in “Your nickname” (for example anna_river; this helps avoid duplicates) and choose “Listening test”. Use 2–32 characters, starting with a letter or number; letters, numbers, spaces, dots, hyphens and underscores are accepted. Do not enter your surname or email. Keep the same nickname throughout your participation.

The general site is /. Use your assigned link when one has been provided.

Understanding the negative to positive numbers

Valence and arousal each allow every whole number from −100 to +100, including zero: 201 possible positions. The ruler shows reference marks; you can choose values between them. These are degrees of your impression, not percentages, grades of correctness or audio measurements.

For valence, −100 is the negative end, 0 is emotionally neutral, and +100 is the positive end. For arousal, −100 is the passive or low-energy end, 0 is moderate energy, and +100 is the active or high-energy end. For example, −50 lies halfway between the lower endpoint and zero. A value of +25 lies a quarter of the way from zero to the upper endpoint. There are no separate official verbal labels for every intermediate value.

The zero button for arousal may say “Choose moderate (0)” in Listening and “Choose neutral (0)” in a live mode. In both cases it selects the middle of the energy scale, not necessarily an emotionless voice.

An answer must be selected explicitly

“Not rated” means there is no answer yet, even when the handle appears in the centre. Move the slider, or press its zero/midpoint button when that is your answer. On a focused slider, arrow keys move by one permitted step. Do not use the midpoint to mean “I could not hear it”; report a playback problem instead. A replay is allowed, and no score is expected to match a system setting.

Listening test from start to completion

Step 1 Read the setup and give consent

On “Before you begin”, enter or check “Your nickname”. The website asks you to confirm that you are 18 or older, understand the study and agree to storage of your nickname (which includes your first name) and responses; that you comfortably understand spoken English; and that you are using headphones in a quiet place.

Answer “I have already tried Test Mode or Explore Mode in ECHO” honestly. If it applies, tell the researcher before proceeding. Click “Begin listening” when the required confirmations are true.

If “Your session is saved on this device” appears instead, choose “Continue session” to resume your existing participation. An earlier saved session may still show 27 clips. Finish that assigned session and tell the researcher; do not restart merely to enter the new 45-clip cohort. Do not create a second session to replace an unfinished one without telling the researcher.

Step 2 Play the complete clip

On “Clip 1 of 45”, press the audio player's Play control. Let the recording finish at normal speed. The initial rating controls become available after complete playback. You may replay the clip. Keep your volume stable and judge the voice you hear, not your current mood.

Step 3 Answer the first three questions

Rate perceived valence, arousal and naturalness using the questions and grades in Listening questions and response grades. Make an explicit choice for each. These questions concern your impression before you know the intended emotion. Do not navigate to another mode to inspect the engines.

Step 4 Save and reveal the intended emotion

Click “Save these ratings & reveal the target”. Read the target displayed under “THE INTENDED EMOTION WAS”. Your first three answers are now locked. A difference between your impression and the target is a valid observation; do not try to make the earlier answers agree with the label.

Step 5 Rate the match and move on

Answer “How well does the voice express that emotion?” Choose a value from 1 to 5 in half steps, or click “Choose moderately (3)” if that is your judgement. Click “Save & next clip”. Repeat Steps 2–5 for all 45 clips. Each clip requires four answers in total. Take a short break after clips 15 and 30 if needed. Keep the page open and your volume unchanged. Allow roughly 20–30 minutes for Listening; this is a planning estimate and actual time varies.

Step 6 Confirm completion and receipt

After the final clip, you will see “Thank you for listening.” Wait until the status confirms “Received by the researcher: 45 of 45 responses” and that all responses have been received. Listening completion and database receipt are separate events.

If the status says responses are waiting to be sent, keep the page open, reconnect and use “Retry saving”. “Download my copy (CSV)” is an optional backup. Do not use “Download & start a new session on this device” to repeat your participation.

Tell the researcher that you have finished and report any technical problems. Continue to Test or Explore only if assigned. You can then select the corresponding mode button in the workspace or use “← Home” and the activity card.

Listening questions and response grades

Question 1 Perceived valence

“How positive or negative does the voice sound?”

Use whole numbers from −100 to +100. The exact labels are −100 “Very negative”, 0 “Neutral” and +100 “Very positive”. Choose the position that best describes the voice's emotional tone. Negative does not mean a poor-quality recording. To select zero explicitly, click “Choose neutral (0)”.

Question 2 Perceived arousal

“How active or passive does the voice sound?”

Use whole numbers from −100 to +100. The exact labels are −100 “Very passive”, 0 “Moderate” and +100 “Very active”. Judge the overall energy conveyed by the voice. A passive voice is not automatically negative; an active voice is not automatically positive. Click “Choose moderate (0)” when the midpoint is your answer.

Question 3 Naturalness

“How natural does the speech sound?” Select one of the five options.

GradeDisplayed optionHow to interpret it
1Very unnaturalThe delivery sounds strongly artificial or unlike ordinary human speech.
2UnnaturalThe delivery sounds unnatural overall, less strongly than grade 1.
3NeitherNeither natural nor unnatural overall; not a substitute for a missing answer.
4NaturalThe delivery sounds natural overall.
5Very naturalThe delivery sounds very natural and human-like.

Naturalness is separate from emotion match. A voice may express its target clearly while sounding artificial, or sound natural while conveying little of the target.

Question 4 Match after the target is revealed

“How well does the voice express that emotion?” Use the revealed target for this question.

Permitted gradeMeaning on the displayed scale
1Not at all.
1.5 · 2 · 2.5Increasing match between “Not at all” and “Moderately”; 2 is halfway between 1 and 3.
3Moderately.
3.5 · 4 · 4.5Increasing match between “Moderately” and “Very well”; 4 is halfway between 3 and 5.
5Very well.

There are nine permitted positions. The intermediate positions have no additional official verbal labels. Half steps allow an in-between judgement; for example, 3.5 is between 3 and 4. Move the slider or click “Choose moderately (3)”, then “Save & next clip”.

Test mode instructions

Read this section after Listening. Test mode speaks exactly the text you enter. It does not generate a conversational reply. Complete all four emotions in each of the five engines. The required block contains 20 emotion-preset messages and five neutral-control messages, 25 messages in total. Engine names and targets are visible, so these are separate application observations.

Follow these numbered steps

  1. Choose “Test mode”, check your nickname and read the storage statement. Confirm consent if you agree. Keep the same nickname as in Listening. Use the supplied phrase; do not enter personal information.
  2. In “Text to speak”, enter “The package is on the table near the window.” Keep these exact words throughout the block. Choose the first engine in the checklist below, or follow the researcher’s assigned engine order.
  3. Select Happy under “01 Choose the emotion”. Open “Explore individual controls”, set “Control condition” to “Emotion preset”, then click “Generate voice”. Check the card’s engine, TARGET and condition. Use the dropdown and Generate voice for every task; comparison shortcuts may reuse an earlier target.
  4. Play the whole message at normal speed. Open “Rate this voice message”, answer all four voice questions in the next section, and click “Save this message rating”. Wait for “Rating received by the database”. Mark the Happy cell complete only after receipt.
  5. With the same engine and phrase, repeat Steps 3–4 for Angry, then Sad, then Relaxed. Explicitly select each quadrant and keep “Emotion preset”. If the researcher provides a different emotion order, cover the same four cells in that order.
  6. Add one baseline for this engine: select Happy, set “Control condition” to “Neutral controls”, and click Generate voice. Listen and save all four ratings. The match question still refers to Happy; neutral controls are not a fifth emotion quadrant. Restore “Emotion preset” immediately afterwards.
  7. Repeat Steps 2–6 for each remaining engine. Check that every row below has four completed emotion cells and one neutral baseline. Keep the phrase fixed and rate each message before generating another. Do not regenerate a successful output to obtain a preferred result.
  8. Confirm 20 emotion-preset ratings plus five neutral ratings, all received. Check “Messages, recordings and submitted ratings saved.” Report unavailable cells and technical failures honestly; a failed generation does not count as a completed condition. Take breaks between engines and give the researcher the checklist and any incidents.

Voice questions in Test and Explore

Both live modes show the following four questions inside “Rate this voice message”. Listen to the whole message before submitting. The labels differ slightly from Listening; follow the labels in the mode you are using.

“How positive or negative does the voice sound?”

−100 “Negative” → 0 “Neutral” → +100 “Positive”. Every whole number is available. The button “Choose neutral (0)” explicitly selects zero. Interpret the direction and distance from zero as explained in Key terms and how to use the scales.

−100 “Low energy” → 0 “Neutral” → +100 “High energy”. Every whole number is available. Here “Neutral” means the middle of the energy scale. Use “Choose neutral (0)” if this is your judgement.

Save and check

Click “Save this message rating” after explicitly selecting all four answers. “Your voice message rating” replaces the form heading after submission. “Rating saved in this browser; waiting for the database” is not final confirmation. Wait for “Rating received by the database”, using “Retry saving” if necessary. A missing or inaudible message should be reported, not assigned an invented midpoint score.

Explore Mode instructions

Explore writes a response and voices it using the selected emotional instruction. Complete all four emotions with all three engines: 12 required conversations, with one successful reply in each. Start fresh for every engine–emotion cell so earlier conversation history does not carry across targets. This minimum checks response integration; it does not establish long-conversation performance.

Follow these numbered steps

  1. After Listening, choose “Explore Mode”. Check your nickname and storage consent. Choose the first engine in “Conversation TTS engine”: Chatterbox, Parler-TTS or Kokoro according to the checklist or researcher-assigned order. The menu is not a ranking.
  2. Start a fresh conversation. Select Happy in “Choose the emotion” and explicitly select the planned engine. The engine locks on the first Send, including while generation is in progress. Your selected target is an instruction; you do not have to feel that emotion.
  3. Enter “Help me prepare for a busy study day.” and click “Send”. Use this exact opening prompt in every required conversation. Wait for the reply; do not repeatedly click Send, refresh, or regenerate a successful response to select a favourite.
  4. Listen to the complete voice message at normal speed. If autoplay does not start, press Play. Keep “Show transcript” closed. Open “Rate this voice message”, answer the four voice questions, and save. Wait for “Rating received by the database”.
  5. Click “Show transcript”. Read the reply beside your prompt and the selected target. Complete the three external per-reply questions and the brief experience questions in the Additional questions section. These external answers are not saved by the website. Record the task ID; the researcher links it to the exact conversation and turn IDs.
  6. Confirm “Messages, recordings and submitted ratings saved” before pressing “New conversation”; that button clears the visible chat. Repeat Steps 2–5 with Angry, then Sad, then Relaxed, keeping the same engine and opening prompt. Re-select the engine and emotion after every reset. Follow an assigned alternate order if provided.
  7. Repeat the four new conversations for each of the other two engines. Check all 12 cells below. An unanswered, failed or inaudible reply does not count; record the incident and let the researcher decide any retry. Do not substitute a different engine.
  8. Finish after all required ratings have receipts and the researcher has your external answers and incident notes. Take breaks between engines. Optional follow-up dialogue is recorded separately, after this block, and does not replace the 12 cells. Each conversation allows at most ten exchanges and one TTS engine.

Additional questions for the application activities

Answer these questions on the external coverage sheet, which is not saved by the website. Use your study code and task ID. Complete one set for each of the 12 Explore cells; the researcher links the exact message IDs.

After Test mode

The researcher may ask these two open questions. Answer in your own words; there is no numerical grade.

  1. What, if anything, made the expression easier or harder to judge?
  2. Was any output difficult to understand or uncomfortable to listen to? Identify the message if possible.

After each of the twelve required Explore replies

Reveal the transcript only after submitting the voice ratings, then answer each question separately.

  1. “How well does the written reply address your request?” Consider whether it answers the particular request. This is relevance, not voice quality.
  2. “How well does the emotional tone of the written reply fit the selected target?” Consider the words and phrasing in relation to Happy, Angry, Sad or Relaxed, as selected for this reply.
  3. “How consistent is the emotional tone of the voice with the emotional tone of the written reply?” Consider whether the two expressions fit together. They can agree with each other while both missing the selected target.

After each required Explore conversation

Use the same 1–5 grades, or Cannot judge, for each question:

  1. “How appropriate was the voice for this conversation?”
  2. “How helpful was the reply for completing the task?”
  3. “How comfortable was the waiting time?”

Then answer these open questions in your own words:

  1. “What worked well in this conversation?”
  2. “Was there a point where the words and the voice did not fit together, or did not fit the selected target? If so, identify the turn and describe it in your own words.”
  3. “What one change would most improve the experience?”

Other terms and help when something goes wrong

This reference section is for the application activities after Listening. You do not need technical expertise to participate.

Terms visible in the live interface

TermMeaning and what you need to do
Russell quadrantsFour regions combining positive/negative valence with high/low arousal. The emotion quadrants are the project's practical choices in these regions.
Emotion presetA prepared configuration intended to express the selected target. Its effectiveness is what people are evaluating.
Neutral controlsThe engine's baseline condition. It may still sound expressive; report what you hear.
Speaking rateHow quickly speech is delivered. A displayed multiplier describes a system setting, not a required rating.
Pitch controlA setting related to how high or low the voice sounds. Pitch alone does not determine your emotion judgement.
Output gainA setting that changes the audio level. Keep your own listening volume unchanged for comparison.
Emotional coherenceHow well the requested emotion and the response's expression fit. The questionnaire separates target match, naturalness and agreement between text and voice.
Exchange or turnYour message and the system's reply. The Explore counter shows completed exchanges, up to ten.
Database receiptConfirmation that the research server has received a record; different from a browser backup.
CSV and WAVCSV is a tabular data copy; WAV is an audio file. Downloading either does not submit or replace a rating.

If audio or rating controls do not work

Check the player and headphone output, then replay the complete message at normal speed. Rating controls remain disabled until playback finishes. If “Not rated” remains, move each slider or use its midpoint button. Report unresolved problems; do not guess answers.

If generation is slow or fails

Keep the page open while progress or queue information is displayed. “Cancel” stops a pending generation. A failed Explore attempt can show “No exchange was counted. Your message is kept so you can retry.” Report the failure and follow the researcher's instructions before retrying or changing engines.

If saving is pending or you need a break

Reconnect, select “Retry saving” and wait for receipt. Keep browser data. Listening resumes with “Continue session” in the same browser. Live chat and unsent answers may not reappear after a reload; finish ratings before leaving and report any lost view.

To withdraw, follow your participant information sheet. “Delete my ratings” in Listening removes its linked ratings after confirmation; a minimal marker prevents re-upload. Contact the researcher about exported copies, application records and external sheets. “New conversation” does not withdraw data.

Finish when the assigned questions are accounted for, submitted ratings have confirmed receipt, and the researcher has your external sheet and incident details.
