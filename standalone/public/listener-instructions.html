ECHO Listener Guide

Why listeners are needed

Automatic testing and scoring of synthetic speech exist. There are classifiers that label the emotion in an audio clip, and models that score how naturally a voice sounds. However, the fact of whether the intended emotion reaches a listener, ultimately depends on human perception of a voice sound.

Your ratings are the reference benchmark against the automatic measures that are checked. Where they agree with you, they can be trusted for the rest of the corpus. Where they disagree, the automatic figure is the one that gets discarded.

You do not need experience with speech technology. Report your own experience, including uncertainty and problems. There are no “correct” ratings. A voice may sound different from its intended emotion; that is useful information.

ECHO does not need your microphone and does not detect your emotions. You enter text and choose settings yourself. The interface and the language of test is English.

The study is deliberately small dedicated to a handful of careful listeners. Each listener hearing the full set under controlled conditions is worth more here than a large crowd rating a few clips each.

5. Make sure you can understand spoken and written English well enough to judge the clips and replies

Step 2. Start Listening test

On the Listening page, read Before you begin. Read the consent information and the statements about English comprehension and headphones. Answer these based on your actual situation.

The statement I have already tried Test Mode or Explore Mode in ECHO must reflect what you have already done, even if you only tried the prototype briefly (optional).

Select the required confirmations only if they are true and you agree to participate. Then select Begin listening.

Keep the same browser and device for Listening. Avoid clearing browser data during the test so you can resume the existing session. Your nickname alone cannot restore a session; keep the participant code for researcher support and do not publish it.

Understanding ratings

Judge the voice that you actually hear

In Listening, first describe the sound without knowing its intended emotion. The first three questions concern the voice’s emotional direction, activity, and naturalness. After you save them, the page reveals the intended emotion and asks a fourth question about the match.

Listen for the way the voice sounds: its tone, pace, expression, and delivery. Do not decide based only on whether the sentence’s meaning is pleasant or unpleasant. Use the whole range when it reflects what you hear; no particular distribution or average is expected.

The first three Listening questions

Question on the pageRangeMeaning of the anchors
How positive or negative does the voice sound?Whole numbers from −100 to +100−100: Very negative; 0: Neutral; +100: Very positive.
How active or passive does the voice sound?Whole numbers from −100 to +100−100: Very passive; 0: Moderate; +100: Very active.
How natural does the speech sound?Whole numbers from 1 to 51: Very unnatural; 2: Unnatural; 3: Neither; 4: Natural; 5: Very natural.

Valence means the voice’s positive or negative emotional quality. Negative emotion does not mean poor audio quality. Arousal means energy or activity here, not sexual arousal. Its midpoint, 0 Moderate, means a moderate level of energy, not necessarily no emotion. Naturalness describes how much the delivery resembles natural spoken speech. Judge these separately.

The question after the target is revealed

How well does the voice express that emotion? uses a 1–5 scale and allows half steps: 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, and 5. The labeled anchors are 1 Not at all, 3 Moderately, and 5 Very well. Rate how well the heard voice expresses the emotion shown on the screen.

An untouched slider is not an answer

The page can show Not rated even when a slider handle appears to sit in the middle. You must deliberately answer each question. If your answer is exactly the middle value, move the slider away and back to that value so the page records your choice. Check the displayed number and that it is no longer marked Not rated.

Do not use a middle score as a substitute for missing audio. If you cannot hear the clip, pause and resolve or report the problem. Similarly, do not copy another person’s answers or change your impression because you think a particular result is expected.

Listening RULES

Repeat this sequence for each of the 45 clips (3-7 seconds each).

1 Check the current clip

2 Play the full recording

3 Answer the three initial questions

4 Save your initial impression

5 Read the intended emotion

6 Rate the match

7 Save and move on

8 Take the planned breaks

Listening completion and resume

Confirm the whole session was received

At the end, look for Thank you for listening. Then check Received by the researcher: 45 of 45 responses. Reaching the final screen and having all responses received are both important. If the received count is lower or saving is still pending, use Retry saving when offered and check the count again.

If responses remain pending after retrying, preserve the page, record the message and count, and contact the researcher. Do not mark the session complete.

Download my copy (CSV) optionally saves a copy as a table file. It does not replace the receipt confirmation or submission of external feedback.

Continue after closing or refreshing

Return in the same browser on the same device. If Continue session appears, use it to resume your existing Listening session. Check the displayed progress before proceeding. Do not intentionally restart the study to replace your original impressions.

Do not select Download & start a new session on this device to repeat the test. That action starts a new session and is different from continuing the current one. If the prototype cannot find your session, note what happened and ask the researcher how to proceed.

Stop here until Listening is complete

Step 3. Test Mode setup

Test Mode lets you hear the same sentence with different voices and settings. Complete few test, per each engine. An engine is the speech system selected in the menu; no technical knowledge is needed.

If Test Mode is assigned, open it after Listening is complete, or follow the researcher’s assigned route. Read and complete the consent shown for the interactive modes. This is separate from the Listening consent. The information explains that the prototype stores your nickname, messages, audio, settings, generation details, and ratings.

Use exactly the same sentence

In Text to speak, enter 1-2 sentences, i.e.:

The meeting has been moved to Thursday. I will see you there.

Choose the engine and emotion

The TTS engine menu contains the five engines used in the required comparison: Kokoro, Chatterbox, StyleTTS2, CosyVoice2, and Parler-TTS.

Use Choose the emotion to select one of the four targets:

Map labelTargetMeaning on the map
Q1HappyPositive and intense
Q2AngryNegative and intense
Q3SadNegative and calm
Q4RelaxedPositive and calm

These are intended targets, not predicted results or recommended ratings. Check SELECTED TARGET after selecting an emotion. You do not have to feel that emotion yourself.

Voice ratings in Test Mode

Test Mode shares the form Rate this voice message.

Question on the pageAllowed answersLabeled anchors
How positive or negative does the voice sound?Nine positions: −100, −75, −50, −25, 0, 25, 50, 75, 100−100 Negative; 0 Neutral; 100 Positive.
How much energy does the voice transmit?The same nine positions−100 Low energy; 0 Neutral; 100 High energy.
How natural does the voice sound?1–5 in half steps1 Very artificial; 3 Moderate; 5 Very natural.
How closely does it match your chosen emotion?1–5 in half steps1 Not at all; 3 Moderately; 5 Very closely.

Listen to the whole recording. Rate that particular message and judge naturalness, energy, emotional direction, and target match separately.

If a slider says Not rated, it has not been answered. To choose a middle value deliberately, move away from it and back. Check that all four questions have recorded your intended values, then select Save this message rating.

After submission, the form is replaced or summarized under Your voice message rating. Review before saving; if you later notice a mistake, note the task and tell the researcher rather than generating another response to conceal or overwrite it.

Look for Rating received by the database on the message. Rating saved in this browser; waiting for the database means that delivery is still pending. A locally saved answer is not yet a confirmed received answer.

The overall status Messages, recordings and submitted ratings saved. is useful when leaving a conversation. Check it as well as the individual rating receipt. If a status remains pending, keep the page open, check your connection, and select Retry saving when offered.

Step 3. Explore Mode

Explore Mode writes a reply and speaks it aloud. If this stage is assigned, complete a few separate conversations, with one successful assistant reply per conversation. Each tests one engine and emotion.

1. Open Explore Mode.

2. Check your nickname and the interactive storage consent. Start a fresh conversation for the first task and check the 0 / 10 counter.

3. In Conversation TTS engine, select the engine listed in the checklist: Chatterbox, Parler-TTS, or Kokoro.

4. Select the listed emotion in Choose the emotion.

5. Start your conversation with any prompt. Recommended to start with standard dialogue phrases for conversation.

The engine becomes fixed for that conversation as soon as you first select Send, including while the request is still processing. Choose the correct engine before sending. To test another engine, use New conversation after saving the current task.

You will first judge the sound, then read the transcript. Do not open Show transcript before listening and saving the four voice ratings. The interface may allow early opening, so this order depends on you following the procedure. If you open it early by mistake, record this for that task; do not hide it by silently repeating the conversation.
